SECTION 0. VALIDATION OF THE GPT PRD v2.0 (AGENTS, ROLES, PLAN, PHASES) AND CHANGE SUMMARY
======================================================================

I read the whole GPT document (about 1,700 lines). Its product sections (1 to 18) are a loose summary that regresses against the decisions already made. Its governance sections (19 to 22) have good ideas and real defects. This PRD keeps Part A (the product spec approved through v6.4) and rebuilds Part B from the good parts of GPT's governance.

0.1 Strengths kept
- No agent approves its own work, and the Worker cannot self-approve.
- A default-deny release gate that must cite a specific unmet criterion.
- Reviewer chain by task type, a task template with IDs, acceptance criteria and evidence, and severity levels where Critical and High block approval.
- Rework loop, per-phase exit criteria, property-based tests and reproducibility tests.
- Engine before dashboards. Testing from the first phase, not at the end.
- Open decisions listed before development starts.

0.2 Defects in the GPT governance
G1 [Critical] Reviewers are "agents" but independence is only declared. Reviewers from the same model, context and prompt share blind spots and tend to agree with the author. No rule makes review independent by construction.
G2 [Critical] No anti-gaming controls. A Worker could edit tests, loosen tolerances, special-case expected values, or touch the oracle. Nothing protects tests, oracle, tolerances or gate scripts.
G3 [Critical] Agents self-report test results. "Tests passed" must never be accepted from the author. The gate must re-run everything itself from a clean checkout.
G4 [High] The oracle is not defined as an independent, blind build. The Algorithm Developer "provides independent test fixtures" for its own code, which is not independent.
G5 [High] The schedule validator and KPI recalculation have no separate owner, so the same agent could write both generator and validator.
G6 [High] Phases are horizontal (module by module). The first end-to-end result (an actual minimum HC) arrives only at Phase 8, so integration risk is discovered late. The hardest module (full blended switching, Phase 6) sits before staffing search and roster, delaying the target.
G7 [High] Phase order contradicts its own release plan: Release 1 is "voice-first" but Phase 6 builds full blended operation before Phase 7 sizing.
G8 [High] No human authority. The business owner has no defined decision rights, and no path resolves agent disagreement or a deadlocked reject cycle.
G9 [High] The same six-step review chain applies to every task, including trivial ones, across roughly 130 tasks. This creates bottlenecks and review fatigue, and reviewers start rubber-stamping.
G10 [Medium] Missing roles: Program Orchestrator (task graph, evidence tracking, WIP limits), Test Engineer, Security and Build Engineer, Technical Writer, Oracle Engineer, Validator Developer. The Chief Architect carries too many duties (design, integration, debt, approval).
G11 [Medium] "Technical Reviewer" and "Domain Reviewer" appear in GPT 21.4 but are never defined. GPT 19.3 "Agent authority matrix" contains one sentence and no matrix. GPT 20.4 "Recommended specialist skill assignments" is an empty heading. A chat note ("My final recommendations") is left inside the document.
G12 [Medium] Skills are generic lists with no way to verify an agent actually has them. No calibration (for example seeded-defect exercises) and no measure of reviewer effectiveness.
G13 [Medium] Evidence is not defined as an artifact set, and sign-offs are not bound to the exact version reviewed, so a sign-off can survive a later change.
G14 [Medium] Phase 0 spikes have no pass or fail criteria or fallback decisions. Requirements freeze (P0-T01) has no change control afterward. No requirement identifier scheme exists in the document itself.
G15 [Medium] Final UAT happens only at Phase 12. The business owner sees nothing real until the end.
G16 [Low] No governance metrics (defect escape rate, review detection rate, rework cycles), and no policy on real operational data in agent workspaces.

0.3 Conflicts between the GPT PRD and your decisions (overridden here)
- Release 1 "voice-first": your decision is the target chain with voice, chat, email and back-office dedicated pools, forecast module built last within Release 1.
- Default 5x5 sensitivity levels with all 25 scenarios simulated: conflicts with no hardcoded numbers. Sensitivity is Release 2 with user-defined levels.
- "Negatives and duplicates must not be removed without a user-approved policy": you asked for removal. Kept as logged, confirmed removal (4.3).
- Erlang X retained as an option: excluded. Female restrictions by name: generalized to attribute-based rules (Release 2).
- The GPT product sections omit the locked flow, fidelity gating, per-stage fingerprints, the horizon-level target semantics, minimum seats while open, HC ceiling, statistical decision procedure, and the math corrections found in earlier rounds. Part A restores them.

0.4 What v7.0 added
Part B: specialist roles plus the human Product Owner, independence-by-construction rules, anti-gaming controls, risk-tiered review, a deterministic evidence gate, vertical-slice phases with business demos, change control and program metrics. Part A was carried from v6.4 with the holdout pack and governance links added.

0.5 What v7.1 changed (Part B)
1. Every role has an agent card: mission, authority, prohibitions, allowed and forbidden inputs, outputs, skills, and a checklist procedure. A standard prompt skeleton (19.3) makes each card directly usable as an agent instruction.
2. The Challenger is a team of seven: a Challenge Lead and six domain challengers (math, WFM operations, software and security, data, roster, process red team). They work blind and in parallel, and every finding needs a reproducible counterexample (19.9).
3. Auditors are split into a code and algorithm auditor and a platform, security and data auditor (19.8).
4. A skills registry (Section 26) gives every skill an ID, a definition and a proof exercise. Every role and every task lists the skill IDs it needs.
5. Every task is a task card with requirement references, dependencies, specification owner, implementer, verifiers, reviewers, challengers, skills, inputs, outputs, acceptance tests and attack focus (Section 23).
6. Communication contracts (Section 27) define the Task Packet, Evidence Package, Finding Report, Attack Log, Sign-off and Escalation formats.
7. The task state machine, the attack taxonomy (A01 to A12) and the challenger coverage rule are explicit (21.3, 19.9).

======================================================================
SECTION 1. TARGET AND DEFINITIONS
======================================================================

1.1 Target
The app computes the real minimum HC that achieves the configured KPI targets over the full FCT horizon, based on the FCT volume and AHT, subject to the configured schedule rules and constraints, and shows how that number is built up from the pure business requirement. In Fixed-HC mode it reports the KPIs that a given HC or roster achieves.

1.2 Definitions
Horizon: the full period covered by the confirmed FCT. Interval: the FCT resolution (15, 30 or 60 minutes), as declared.
Seat: one agent available to take work during an interval. Seat-hours: seats multiplied by interval length, summed.
KPI target scope: each target is evaluated once over the horizon, as a ratio of sums.
Pure requirement (Layer 1): the minimum seat-hours that achieve the horizon KPIs when seats can be placed freely by interval, with no schedule rules and no shrinkage. Shown as seat-hours, peak seats and FTE-equivalent (derived, not a headcount).
Roster HC (Layer 2): the minimum number of unique agents whose schedules obey every hard rule and whose simulated horizon KPIs pass at the chosen fidelity.
Gross HC (Layer 3): Roster HC adjusted for the residual shrinkage that the roster does not already model.
Mode A (Find Min HC): Layers 1, 2 and 3. Mode B (Fixed HC): given HC or roster, achieved KPIs.
Fidelity: Quick (analytic only, no simulation, "Estimated"), Screened (simulation, sequential stopping allowed, "non-certified"), Certified (full decision procedure of Section 11).
Requirement identifiers: every numbered item in this PRD (for example 8.4.2 or 13.5) is a requirement identifier used by traceability (14.1, 24.2).

1.3 Bridge (Mode A)
Pure requirement (FTE-equivalent), then paid time and breaks, then shift shape, then weekly rules, then rounding and simulation adjustment = Roster HC, then shrinkage = Gross HC. Each step shows its delta and cause (8.8).

======================================================================
SECTION 2. GOVERNING RULES
======================================================================

2.1 Number Policy
N1. Every numeric value belongs to exactly one class: U uploaded data; P user parameter (editable, with unit, range, validation, stored in every snapshot); D derived (formula and inputs shown); K named constant (mathematical, unit-conversion, calendar or algorithm constant, with provenance and a pinning test); R result.
N2. The source may contain only 0, 1 and named K constants. A dependency-free build-time scan fails the build on any other numeric literal. K constants appear in an in-app Constants Register.
N3. No default operational or statistical values. Required fields start empty. "Insert example" fills editable cells marked "example", and the run is blocked until each is confirmed or replaced. User-created templates (4.10) are user data, not defaults.
N4. Engine-control parameters are P-class, visible in an Engine Parameters panel, stored in every snapshot, and belong to the stage that uses them (3.4).
N5. No silent transformation. Clipping, truncation, rounding, imputation, outlier handling, exclusion, removal and aggregation each report count, magnitude and effect on the mean, and each is a switch or parameter. Removal of negative values and duplicates at upload (4.3) is automatic but always logged row by row and confirmed by the user.
N6. Stored and exported values keep full precision. Display rounding is a visible setting. Every ceiling and floor is labelled in the lineage.
N7. Every result links to its formula, inputs, parameters, engine hash, data hash and seeds (stored at result-record level).
N8. Hitting any limit (iteration cap, seat cap, HC ceiling, solver size cap, memory cap, row cap, removed-share limit, precision not achieved) is an explicit warning or error, never a silent answer.
N9. No NaN or Infinity may cross a module boundary (13.11).

2.2 Zero-Dependency Policy
Z1. One HTML file. No libraries, runtime fetches, CDN, remote fonts or images. The build fails on any external URL, external module import, fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket or sendBeacon.
Z2. Only browser built-ins (DOM, Web Workers, Blob, IndexedDB, typed arrays, TextEncoder and TextDecoder, Intl), each feature-detected at startup and shown in an Environment panel (13.16).
Z3. No Math.random and no crypto.subtle. The engine has its own keyed generator and hash with published test vectors.
Z4. All numerical and statistical routines are in-source (normal and t quantiles by numerical inversion, incomplete beta, exact binomial bounds, distribution sampling, QR and IRLS, revised simplex, golden-section search), each verified against external oracle fixtures that the engine never reads.
Z5. Build and test scripts are dependency-free plain scripts. They are development tools, not part of the shipped file.

2.3 Information Discipline
A dispatch or routing decision may use only information observable at that moment (arrival time, queue, category, deadline, elapsed waiting, configured expected handling time, agent state). It may not use realized service time, realized patience or future arrivals. A forecast backtest may use only data dated on or before its forecast origin, plus calendar information known in advance. The self-test fails if either rule is broken.

======================================================================
SECTION 3. LOCKED FLOW, BLOCKS AND FIDELITY
======================================================================

3.1 Modes
Mode A Find Min HC. Mode B Fixed HC. Chosen at run setup. Both share S1 to S5.

3.2 Fidelity
Chosen at run setup and upgradable later. An upgrade creates new results, and earlier ones stay as read-only history. Quick runs the Erlang or workload estimate and the LP roster only. Screened and Certified run the simulation. A stage is Done when completed at the chosen fidelity. Every result screen and export shows the fidelity label. Quick results are called "estimated", never "achieved".

3.3 Stages (a finite state machine; every transition is enumerated and tested)
S1 Upload FCT. Done when the file is parsed, columns mapped and confirmed (4.1), formats confirmed (13.4), the cleaning report (4.4) shown and confirmed, and any gaps created by cleaning have an explicit treatment. Re-mapping or re-cleaning after S1 is Done creates a new data fingerprint and marks every later stage Stale.
S2 History. Done when history is uploaded, mapped, cleaned and confirmed the same way, or the user clicks an explicit "Skip history".
S3 Forecast confirmation. With history, the app shows the comparison (5.6) and the user picks one forecast ("Use my FCT" or "Use app forecast"). Without history, the user's FCT is locked directly. Done when a Forecast Release is locked. Only the locked forecast enters the engine.
S4 Run setup and configuration. Mode, fidelity, and the three blocks (3.4). Done when every required field is filled, no example value is unconfirmed, and validation is clean.
S5 Estimate. Erlang (real-time pools) and workload floor (deferrable pools). Done when computed with no blocking warning.
Mode A: S6 Layer 1 Pure requirement. S7 Layer 2 Schedule (roster generation, independent validation, Roster HC; or ended at the HC ceiling with best-achievable KPIs, 8.9). S8 Layer 3 and Results.
Mode B: S6B Roster build or evaluation (Section 10). S7B Achievements and Results.
A stage may also end Done as "declared infeasible with a named cause".

3.4 Configuration blocks, run controls and dependencies
Business block: pools and queues, horizon KPI targets, service-time and patience inputs, productivity factor per pool, minimum seats while open, occupancy cap, optional guardrails, warm-up, pre-roll, drain window, time quantum.
Schedule block: roster rules (4.8), roster cycle length, HC search ceiling, and (Mode B) the given HC or uploaded roster.
Shrinkage block: shrinkage components, contract hours, productive hours per FTE.
Run controls (one set per stage): fidelity, replication counts, seed blocks, tolerances, iteration caps, solver and heuristic parameters. Display settings affect nothing and stale nothing.
Dependencies: Layer 1 depends on Forecast Release, Business block, Layer 1 run controls. Layer 2 depends on Layer 1, Schedule block, Layer 2 run controls. Layer 3 depends on Layer 2 and the Shrinkage block (instant recompute). Mode B depends on Forecast Release, Business block, Schedule block, Mode B run controls.
Each result stores a fingerprint (13.7) of its inputs and upstream results. A changed fingerprint marks that result and everything downstream Stale.

3.5 Lock rules
Locked stages are visible but disabled, with the reason shown. Stale results stay as read-only history and are never reused. An infeasible result blocks the next stage and names the block to edit. Cancelling leaves the stage Open, and completed replications stay in the cache (13.5). Incomplete work can be saved but cannot advance. Stage states: Locked, Open, In progress, Done, Stale, Blocked. Changing the confirmed forecast creates a new run.

SECTION 4. INPUTS
======================================================================

4.1 Upload files, column mapping and field definitions
4.1.1 Files: FCT (required), history (optional), past forecast (optional), roster (Mode B, optional). All csv.
4.1.2 System fields:
FCT: date, interval start, queue, forecast volume (offered), forecast AHT. Optional: segment. Interval resolution and timezone offset are declared.
History: date, interval start, queue, offered volume, handled volume, AHT (weighted by handled volume). Optional: abandoned volume, segment. If only handled volume exists, the app flags the history as biased low wherever abandonment exists.
Past forecast: date, interval start, queue, forecast volume, actual volume, forecast AHT, actual AHT (used only to measure the accuracy of the user's FCT process).
Roster: agent id, pool, date, shift start, shift end, repeated pairs of break start and break duration.
AHT is volume-weighted whenever aggregated. Each field shows name, required flag, unit or type, and a description.
4.1.3 Column mapping screen (shown before any rows are processed):
- Each system field has a dropdown listing the csv headers, so csv headers need not match system names.
- Suggestions (header comparison after trimming, ignoring case, spaces and underscores, plus a user-editable alias list) are proposals only, never applied until confirmed.
- Required fields must be mapped. One csv column cannot map to two fields. Unmapped columns are ignored and listed.
- A combined timestamp column can be split into date and interval start, with the format confirmed.
- Duplicate headers are shown with column positions ("Volume (col 4)"). A file without header row is supported by position.
- A preview (row count is a parameter) shows raw cell, mapped field and parsed value, with failed cells highlighted.
- Queue values are mapped to configured queues or excluded. Unmapped values block S1.
- A type check reports mapped columns whose cells do not match the expected type.
- Mappings, aliases and queue-value mappings save as templates (4.10), and a matching template is proposed but must be confirmed.
- The user can return to mapping at any time before S1 is Done.

4.2 Original and cleaned data
The original file is never modified. Its hash is stored as the source record. Cleaning produces a derived dataset with its own hash. Both hashes and the mapping are stored in the Forecast Release and in every result's lineage.

4.3 Validation and cleaning rules (fixed order)
1. Parse and type check. Unparseable cells make the row rejected. Rejected rows (bad formats, missing required values, queue mapping errors) are listed and never silently repaired.
2. Remove negative values. Any row with a negative numeric value (volume, handled, abandoned, AHT, duration) is removed. A row with AHT of zero or less where volume is positive is also removed and reported as its own category.
3. Remove duplicates, key (date, interval start, queue, segment if present). Exact duplicates are removed automatically, keeping the first. Conflicting duplicates (same key, different values) need a user-chosen rule before S1 completes: keep first, keep last, or exclude every row of that key. Because negatives are removed first, a negative row cannot conflict with a valid row.
4. Gap detection. After removal, the app lists every interval inside operating hours that now has no row, distinct from zero-volume and closed intervals, including missing dates and intervals already in the file. The user must choose an explicit treatment: leave missing (blocks S1), treat as zero, interpolate, replace with a selected historical average, or fix the file. Nothing is filled silently.
5. Reported, not removed: inconsistent totals, outliers (5.2), queues marked outbound (rejected with a message; out of scope in Release 1).
6. Safety limit. If the share of rows removed exceeds a user-set maximum (no default), S1 is blocked with a warning that a wrong mapping is likely. The user can proceed only by explicit override.
Roster files use the same rules with key (agent id, date, shift start), and the report states the effect on seat-hours.

4.4 Cleaning report (confirmed before S1 completes)
Counts (total, accepted, rejected, removed negatives per field, AHT-zero rows, exact duplicates, conflicting duplicates and rule, gaps and treatment). Effect (volume removed absolute and percent, by queue, and workload effect). Every removed or rejected row with original row number, key fields, value and reason. Downloadable cleaned data, removed rows, rejected rows and full report. Confirmation is recorded with the data fingerprint.

4.5 Calendars
Operating hours, weekly pattern, holidays, Ramadan, Eid and events are user-supplied data only (Islamic dates depend on observation, so none are computed). Calendar overrides can replace allowed shift lengths and weekly-hours range for specified weeks. DST: fixed offset only, ambiguous or missing local times rejected.

4.6 Pools, queues and targets
A pool is a group of agents with identical eligibility serving one or more queues of the same class (real-time or deferrable), with a queue priority parameter. Release 1 models dedicated pools only, so the total Min HC (sum over pools) may overstate a cross-skilled operation, and the result page says so. Per queue, horizon-level targets: SL (SL1, SL2 or SL3), ASA, answer rate, abandonment cap, optional occupancy cap, and for deferrable queues percent by deadline and end-of-horizon backlog. Analytic curves for a multi-queue pool are aggregate (workload-weighted AHT, single class), labelled approximate. The DES is the authority.

4.7 Service-time, patience and productivity inputs
Mean and variability (CV or standard deviation) per queue, or an empirical sample. Mean-only input blocks the run until variability is supplied or the user accepts an "uncalibrated variability" warning. A patience distribution is required for real-time queues (flagged "assumed" if no data). Productivity factor per pool (reference group equals one): effective AHT = AHT x factor. The realized mix-weighted AHT is reported.

4.8 Roster configuration (all P-class, no defaults)
- Allowed shift starts: one list of clock times for everyone (examples only, such as 00:00, 06:00, 07:00, 07:30). The engine may start a shift only at these times. They need not be evenly spaced or on interval boundaries. At least one entry is required.
- Allowed shift lengths: a list (one entry means fixed). Shift end may cross midnight.
- Breaks: count, duration, window, minimum separation, paid flag, counts-toward-contract flag.
- Weekly OFF days and adjacency rule, maximum consecutive working days, minimum rest between shifts, minimum and maximum weekly hours.
- Roster cycle length in weeks (8.2).
- Minimum seats while a queue is open (per pool, required).
- HC search ceiling per pool (required).
- Occupancy cap (optional, horizon level).
- Rule class per rule: Hard (default) or Relaxable.
- Optional guardrails (off by default), used only by 12.6.

4.9 Shrinkage and productive hours
Each component (leave, sickness, training, meetings, absence, adherence, other) has a rate, a stated base, and optionally a rate per week type. Components already modeled in the roster or simulation are excluded, and the UI blocks the double entry. Contract hours per week and paid-hour definitions derive FTE-equivalent.

4.10 Templates and completeness
User-owned templates: save any block, load into a new run, copy from a previous run. Loaded values show "from template" and must be confirmed. A completeness meter lists unfilled required fields by block. Mapping templates hold mappings, aliases, split settings, queue-value mappings and the chosen conflicting-duplicate rule. Templates live in the project container and can be exported and imported.

======================================================================
SECTION 5. FORECAST AND COMPARISON
======================================================================

5.1 When the app forecasts: only when history is uploaded. Without history, the user's FCT is the forecast, and no forecast accuracy exists.

5.2 History cleaning (after upload cleaning): history window start per queue; exclusion records with reasons; outliers by a robust score (median and median absolute deviation) with a threshold parameter, each point kept, excluded or replaced by the fitted value, with counts and effects reported; only complete days enter daily totals; minimum-history rules are parameters.

5.3 Two-stage structure: daily total per queue, then interval allocation by an intraday profile per day type, plus interval AHT.
- Profile by Dirichlet-multinomial shrinkage: p_hat_i = (sum over analog days d of w_d x count_d,i + kappa x p_pool,i) / (sum of w_d x n_d + kappa), recency weights w_d (decay parameter), kappa by method of moments (D-class, displayed, user override allowed), optional smoothing then renormalization.
- Special days use analog days, else the nearest day-type profile flagged "no analog".
- AHT: day-level volume-weighted mean with an intraday shape.
- Coherence: interval volumes sum to day totals and queue totals under a visible rounding policy.

5.4 Methods in Release 1: (a) seasonal naive baseline; (b) weighted analog average; (c) quasi-Poisson regression with log link (trend, day-of-week, day-of-month classes, event windows with lead and lag days), IRLS, dispersion from Pearson residuals, HAC standard errors, unbiased on the original scale, handles zero days. Event effects are estimated only if the event occurred at least the minimum-occurrences parameter times, otherwise the user supplies a multiplier flagged "assumed". Moving Islamic-calendar events are confounded with annual seasonality, so the app warns and reports wider uncertainty.

5.5 Backtest and selection: rolling origin, nested tuning, leakage assertion; WAPE, MAE, signed bias at interval, day and week level by lead bucket and day type, zero denominators counted. A method is recommended only if its out-of-sample loss beats seasonal naive by a paired block-bootstrap interval excluding zero, with Holm correction across methods. Otherwise the baseline is used and labelled.

5.6 Comparison report (history present): app forecast versus the user's FCT by interval, day, week and queue (volume, difference, bias, WAPE between them, AHT and workload difference), largest disagreements ranked, the app forecast's backtested accuracy, and the user's FCT accuracy only if a past-forecast file exists. Otherwise it states that it shows disagreement, not which forecast is right. No simulation is involved.

5.7 Confirmation and Forecast Release: immutable and versioned (chosen forecast by queue and interval, original and cleaned data hashes and mapping, calendar, which forecast was chosen, comparison report, and for the app forecast its method, coefficients and backtest). Every result shows "FCT: yours" or "FCT: app".

SECTION 6. DEMAND GENERATION
======================================================================

6.1 G1 Reconciled (default): interval counts equal the forecast under the rounding policy, keyed uniform timestamps within each interval. G2 Poisson (option): exact Poisson counts by log-domain recurrence from the mode. The mode is shown in results.
6.2 Rounding: carry-forward cumulative rounding preserves the horizon total. A reconciliation table shows expected, generated and difference per queue and interval, for volume and AHT.
6.3 Determinism: every random value is a pure function of its key (seed block, replication, queue, kind, day or interval, rank, attempt). Contact identity is (queue, day, rank). A 53-bit open-interval uniform is built from two mixed 32-bit words. Contacts are generated lazily (13.6).

======================================================================
SECTION 7. LAYER 1: PURE REQUIREMENT
======================================================================

7.1 Hours and valid bounds
Workload W (hours) = sum over horizon of volume x AHT x productivity factor / seconds-per-hour, plus opening WIP for deferrable pools.
phi_min = the smallest fraction of offered contacts that the active horizon targets force to be answered (answer-rate target a gives a; abandonment cap b gives 1 - b; SL1 target tau gives tau; SL2 and SL3 give none unless supplied; with infinite patience and no abandonment target it is one).
Necessary conditions are horizon-level only: total seat-hours must exceed phi_min x W strictly, and be at least phi_min x W / c if an occupancy cap c is set. There are no per-interval floors. Per-interval conditions come only from the minimum seats while open.
FTE-equivalent = pure seat-hours / hours per FTE over the horizon (contract hours per week times weeks, before breaks and shrinkage), labelled "contract-hours basis".

7.2 Erlang module (real-time pools; seed, ranking aid and benchmark)
Erlang B by recurrence: B(0) = 1, B(n) = A x B(n-1) / (n + A x B(n-1)). Erlang C: C = N x B / (N - A x (1 - B)), N > A; SL = 1 - C x exp(-(N - A) x T / AHT); ASA = C x AHT / (N - A). Erlang A (abandonment rate = 1 / mean patience): recurrence under a tolerance parameter, waiting-time distribution numerically, withheld until it matches the DES within pre-registered tolerances. Every Erlang screen shows inputs, formula, assumptions, limitations, warnings. The DES governs. Deferrable pools do not use Erlang: N_min = (workload + opening WIP) / per-agent capacity over the horizon, reduced by the cancellation share if tasks can expire.

7.3 KPI definitions (formulas shown, variants per queue)
SL1 = answered within T / offered. SL2 = same with short abandons removed from the denominator. SL3 = answered within T / answered. ASA, answer rate, abandonment (with and without short abandons). Occupancy = (talk + hold + ACW) / (talk + hold + ACW + ready-idle), over the horizon. Deferrable: percent completed by deadline over the horizon and end-of-horizon WIP. All horizon KPIs are ratios of sums within a replication.

7.4 DES core
Event-driven clock. Times are integer multiples of the time quantum in Float64 (exact to 2^53), so within-replication time sums are exact. Ties break by event-type order, then entity id. Service times: keyed unit-mean standardized variates (for example lognormal with log-variance ln(1 + CV^2)) times AHT and factors; truncation bounds are parameters and the realized mean and CV are reported. Opening state: empty, pre-roll, or user WIP; warm-up truncation parameter, optional MSER. Policies (P-class): shift-end behavior, break-start deferral rule and maximum deferral, adherence offsets, idle-agent criteria, priority weights. Seat-curve mode (Layer 1): capacity changes at interval boundaries, reductions release idle seats first. Replication = one full horizon.

7.5 Real-time mechanics
Dispatch: eligibility, capacity, queue priority. Event queue O(log E). Longest-idle selection O(log A) amortized. Chat concurrency: each chat has remaining effort w; progress rate per chat is 1 / f(k) when k chats are active on that agent (user table); when k changes, remaining effort is advanced and completion events rescheduled with version stamps. Effort accounting and routing fill policy are parameters. Uncalibrated tables warn.

7.6 Deferrable mechanics
Dispatch rule is a parameter (EDF, Latest Safe Start, slack-based), no optimality claimed. Latest Safe Start = deadline minus expected remaining handling on the business calendar (2.3). Cases can be parked and resumed at day close. Idle-agent assignment uses an ordered criteria list (case count, utilization, workload, idle time) with a tolerance band per criterion. Drain window is a parameter, drain consumed is reported, remaining backlog makes the candidate infeasible. A per-case ledger is available through seeded replay.

7.7 Pure requirement solver
1. Gain curves G_i(s) (expected contacts answered within threshold in interval i with s seats) from the minimum seats while open up to the maximum-seat parameter, from Erlang C (s above load), Erlang A (once verified) or budgeted DES finite differences. Without verified Erlang A, ranking is restricted to s above load, flagged "analytic floor in use", and DES descent may remove seats below it. Multi-queue pools use the aggregate curve.
2. Concavity test per curve; the upper concave envelope replaces a curve only where concavity fails (interval flagged).
3. Minimize total seat-hours subject to the horizon KPI rows and the minimum seats while open. Filling seat increments in order of gain per seat-hour solves the analytic problem for concave curves. The ordered list defines a nested path P(k), where P(k+1) contains P(k).
4. UI caveat: a horizon-level optimum may leave intervals near critical load where stationary formulas are least accurate. The DES is the authority.
5. DES calibration: shift each gain curve by its measured per-interval discrepancy and re-solve (iteration count is a run control).
6. DES descent: deterministic local search removing one seat-increment at a time while still passing (budget is a run control).
7. Search and certification follow Section 11 over the path. Quick stops after item 3.
8. Claim: minimal along the path with descent applied. Global minimality is not claimed.

======================================================================
SECTION 8. LAYER 2: SCHEDULE AND ROSTER HC
======================================================================

8.1 Problem: find the smallest HC h per pool such that h agent schedules satisfy every hard rule (4.8), start shifts only at allowed starts, use only allowed lengths, and whose simulated horizon KPIs pass at the chosen fidelity. The independent validator (8.6) must pass. Search is bounded by the HC search ceiling.

8.2 Columns and LP
- A column is one agent's schedule over one roster cycle (cycle length in weeks): starts and lengths from the allowed lists, OFF days, breaks in windows, every rest, consecutive-day and weekly-hours rule obeyed including cycle wrap-around. The horizon roster is the cycle repeated with calendar overrides applied (truncated if the horizon is shorter). The UI states that week-to-week volume variation is absorbed by the horizon KPI and not followed by the roster, and shows coverage versus requirement by week.
- Coverage of an interval by a column is the fraction worked (pro-rata for mid-interval starts), net of the minimum break share forced by break windows, so the LP stays a valid relaxation. The DES uses exact times.
- LP (analytic model): minimize agents subject to coverage_i >= minimum seats while open + seat increments in interval i, and the horizon KPI rows measured by gain-curve segments. The optimum is the "model-based bound".
- Column generation: the pricing problem is a dynamic program over cycle days with state (cycle day, consecutive-work run, previous shift end, OFF days used this week, hours used this week), exact for the modeled rules, run until no schedule has reduced cost below minus the tolerance.
- Scale controls: a size estimator computes rows and expected columns before the run. LU factorization with periodic refactorization. Dense storage only if under the memory cap. Dual stabilization box and tailing-off detection (window and tolerance are run controls). A stalled or capped solve makes the bound "approximate", not valid. Beyond the size cap the bound is "n/a". Status (optimal, stalled, iteration cap, numerical trouble) is reported.

8.3 Rigorous lower bound: HC_rigorous = ceil(minimum seat-hours bound of 7.1 / M), where M is the maximum seat-hours one agent can supply over the horizon under the hard rules (same dynamic program, maximizing seat-hours net of forced break share). It does not depend on Erlang.

8.4 HC search: (1) bracket from the larger bound up to a feasible heuristic roster (round up, diving, seeded multi-start local search), capped at the HC ceiling; (2) for candidate h, build the best roster for h (LP in maximization form, rounded), simulate at Screened settings, bisect; (3) certify with the fixed-sequence descending procedure over h (Section 11); (4) if monotonicity is violated across a sweep, report it and certify the lowest PASS in the unbroken descending chain. Quick stops after the LP roster and heuristic.

8.5 Breaks and closed loop: one break per shift by exact min-cost flow, several breaks by sequential flows (labelled heuristic). Loop with monotone structure: up-phase raises requirements where the DES shows the largest KPI deficit until pass or cap; down-phase tests reductions one at a time from a PASS state and accepts only those staying PASS. States are hashed, repeated states end with "cycle detected", and the best roster is kept.

8.6 Independent schedule validator: a separate module written from the rule text of 4.8, importing nothing from the roster generator, LP or DES, with its own time handling. It recomputes every rule from the raw shift list (allowed starts and lengths, breaks in windows with separation and paid flags, OFF days, maximum consecutive days including cycle wrap, rest gap, weekly hours, minimum seats while open, occupancy cap) and runs on every roster. For generated rosters any Hard violation blocks the result. For an uploaded roster (Mode B) violations are findings only. A KPI recalculation module, also independent, recomputes reported KPIs from simulation event records. Both are cross-checked against the external oracle (14.2).

8.7 Rule classes and constraint impact: each rule is Hard, Relaxable with approval, or Preference. Mandatory rules are never relaxed automatically. Constraint impact re-solves with each relaxable rule relaxed, warm-started from the original solution, so reported HC is never worse. Savings within the solver gap are "within solver uncertainty". The table shows HC saved per rule and an "interaction" row. Attribution depends on order and is not claimed additive.

8.8 Bridge steps (Mode A), cumulative LPs with rule groups added in fixed order: Step 0 pure seat-hours over contract hours per FTE; Step 1 plus paid time and breaks; Step 2 plus shift shape (starts and lengths, daily); Step 3 plus weekly rules (OFF, rest, consecutive days, weekly hours, cycle wrap); Step 4 integer roster from the heuristic (model-based); Step 5 Roster HC at the chosen fidelity, with the difference from step 4 shown as "simulation adjustment". Steps 0 to 4 are labelled "model-based".

8.9 Diagnostics and ceiling: uncovered intervals are named, and the engine never adds a start time on its own. The report names the one extra start time that would save the most heads (from reduced costs, model-based). If no roster is feasible the binding constraint is named. If the HC ceiling is reached without a pass, the stage ends "target not achieved at ceiling", a Mode B evaluation at the ceiling shows best-achievable KPIs and shortfalls, and no Min HC is claimed.

======================================================================
SECTION 9. LAYER 3: SHRINKAGE AND GROSS HC
======================================================================

Gross HC = Roster HC / (1 - residual shrinkage), with denominator and components displayed. Same-base components add; different-base components compound explicitly. With category-specific rates, gross each category, sum, then apply one ceiling. Per-week-type rates are averaged with weights equal to each week's rostered seat-hours (displayed). Anything already modeled in the roster or simulation is excluded. The delta is attributed per component. Monte Carlo reliability is Release 2.

======================================================================
SECTION 10. MODE B: FIXED HC
======================================================================

10.1 Inputs per pool: a rostered HC (roster built for it); a gross HC (converted: rostered = floor(gross x (1 - residual shrinkage)), shown); or an uploaded roster (mapped and cleaned per 4.1 to 4.4; allowed starts and lengths not enforced, validator findings shown).
10.2 Build for a given HC: maximize the horizon KPI gain subject to the number of agents equal to the given HC (LP in maximization form), round, improve by seeded local search. Hard rules and lists apply to generated rosters.
10.3 Evaluation: DES over the full horizon at the chosen fidelity, each KPI tested by Section 11 (PASS, FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE with interval and margin), binding KPI named. Quick reports "estimated" KPIs.
10.4 Outputs: achieved KPIs versus targets and shortfalls; coverage versus requirement by interval, day and week; seat-hours versus pure requirement (from Layer 1 if a matching Mode A result exists, else analytic and labelled); gap to Min HC only if a matching Mode A result exists, otherwise a button runs Mode A upward from the given HC; validator report; information-only distributions of daily and interval SL.
10.5 Flow: S1 to S5, then S6B and S7B. No Layer 1 certification is required.

======================================================================
SECTION 11. STATISTICAL DECISION PROCEDURE
======================================================================

11.1 Each candidate (path index k, HC h, or the given HC) is simulated for R replications of the full horizon. Each active KPI gives values m_1..m_R (ratios of sums). Targets have a direction (higher-is-better such as SL, answer rate; lower-is-better such as abandonment, ASA, occupancy cap).
11.2 Bounds: bound-based (default) uses a one-sided t bound on the across-replication mean at the configured confidence level; if the skew diagnostic exceeds its threshold a studentized bootstrap with keyed resampling replaces it. Probability-based (option): trial unit is one replication, exact binomial bound, derived minimum R (smallest R with P(Bin(R, 1 - p0) <= f) <= alpha; for zero failures R >= ln(alpha) / ln(p0)). If it exceeds R_max the target is "not certifiable".
11.3 Outcomes: per constraint PASS, FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE. Overall PASS needs every active constraint PASS (intersection-union). Overall FAIL when any constraint is FAIL, with Holm correction.
11.4 Seed blocks (search, pilot, certification) are independent. R is the smallest value whose bound half-width is at most the precision parameter (iterated, capped by R_max, precision-not-achieved raised if capped; maximum over constraints). Certification uses a fresh block with fixed R and no peeking. Each re-certification consumes a new block (maximum is a run control), and only the final block supports the reported interval. Screened runs may stop sequentially and are non-certified.
11.5 Certification (fixed-sequence descending): the search and pilot blocks give a candidate c_s. A fresh block evaluates c_s, then the next-lower candidate, and so on in that pre-specified order with the same replications, continuing while PASS and stopping at the first non-PASS. The certified minimum is the lowest candidate in the unbroken PASS chain, with probability of certifying an inadequate candidate at most alpha. If c_s fails, ascend with fresh blocks, then certify with another fresh block.
11.6 Minimality evidence (Mode A): certified HC (or seat-hours) with KPI intervals; the result at one fewer and its failing KPI; the indifference zone; rigorous bound, model-based bound and gap. Wording: "No roster of h minus 1 was found that passes. This is not a proof that none exists." Screened is labelled non-certified, Quick shows only bounds.

SECTION 13. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND SOFTWARE ENGINEERING
======================================================================

13.1 Layers, top to bottom: UI; Orchestrator (flow state machine, fingerprints, job scheduling, store); Worker layer (job runner, replication cache); Engine core (math kernel, keyed generator, statistics, Erlang, DES, solvers, forecast, validator); I/O (CSV, ZIP container, XLSX writer, hash). Dependencies point downward only, the engine core imports nothing from UI, workers, storage or I/O, and circular dependencies are build errors.
13.2 Engine core purity: deterministic functions of (inputs, seeds), no global mutable state, DOM, Date, Math.random, storage or timers. The same core text runs in a Worker, the main thread and the Node test harness (a development tool). A test asserts the core text contains none of the forbidden references.
13.3 Data model and time: typed arrays and plain objects validated against schema at boundaries. Dates are integer day numbers from a documented civil-calendar algorithm (K constants with an oracle test), fixed offset, no Date object in the engine, no localized date parsing. Durations in seconds.
13.4 Ingestion: in-source RFC 4180 parser exposing raw headers to the mapping screen. User-selected delimiter, decimal and thousands separators with preview. Selectable encoding (UTF-8 with BOM handling, windows-1252 and others via TextDecoder). Explicit date format with ambiguity flags, explicit AHT unit with median shown. Cleaning (4.3) runs in a worker in fixed order with original row numbers. Chunked reading, row cap parameter (N8). Typed columns.
13.5 Workers: pool size parameter capped by logical processors; single-thread chunked fallback with warning. No SharedArrayBuffer. Immutable inputs transferred once per worker (memory = input size times workers, shown). A job is (candidate fingerprint, seed block, replication index) returning small aggregates, ledgers only on replay. Progress every K events (parameter). Cancellation terminates and respawns the worker. A replication cache keyed by (candidate fingerprint, seed block, replication index) makes resume identical to an uninterrupted run. Reductions in replication-index order. Structured worker errors, no retry of deterministic failures. JIT canary: fixed exp, log and pow calls cold and warm compared bitwise, with in-source sampling functions engaged if they differ and recorded.
13.6 Memory: contacts generated lazily per interval window, preallocated arrays from estimator capacities, ledgers only on replay, memory cap parameter checked before the run.
13.7 State and fingerprints: one store, immutable result records keyed by fingerprint, engine holds no hidden state. Canonical serialization (sorted keys, UTF-8, shortest round-trip numbers, typed arrays as dtype, length and little-endian bytes). In-source 128-bit non-cryptographic hash with published test vectors, used for engine hash, data hashes and project integrity.
13.8 Project container: a stored ZIP with manifest (schema version, engine hash, entries and hashes, fingerprints), JSON entries for configuration and records, binary typed-array entries. It holds cleaned datasets, removed and rejected rows with the cleaning report, mappings, original-file hashes (original bytes optional). Load validates manifest, sizes, hashes and schema, rejects oversized, truncated or altered files, and migrates step by step with a report.
13.9 Persistence and export: the project file is the system of record; IndexedDB is an autosave cache with atomic snapshots, parameters for interval and number of last-good snapshots, recovery offer after abnormal exit, quota and clear-on-exit risk shown. File System Access used if available, else Blob download. Export CSV and JSON for every table and XLSX for results (own writer, stored ZIP with CRC32, shared strings, 1900 date quirk handled, sheets over the row limit split and reported, cells starting with =, +, - or @ sanitized). XLSX import is Release 2.
13.10 Security: textContent rendering only, no innerHTML on data, no eval or Function. Build-generated CSP: default-src none; script-src by hash; worker-src blob; connect-src none; img-src data. The Environment panel verifies the policy and worker creation. Untrusted files are validated against schema and limits, keys __proto__, constructor and prototype rejected. Nothing is written without a user action, no data leaves the device.
13.11 Errors: structured codes with user message and technical detail, global handlers, finite-value containment at every module boundary, local ring-buffer diagnostics log exportable by the user, no network reporting.
13.12 UI: vanilla JavaScript render functions and event delegation, no heavy main-thread computation (all engine work including Quick and cleaning runs in workers), virtualized tables, in-source canvas or SVG charts, keyboard navigation, labels, contrast, no color-only status, string table for all UI text, Intl formatting with explicit locale. Right-to-left is Release 2.
13.13 Build: modular source assembled by a dependency-free deterministic script. The build embeds the engine hash, file hash and CSP script hash (shown in an About panel) and runs scans: number policy, external references, forbidden APIs in the engine core, innerHTML on data, circular dependencies, requirement coverage.
13.14 Self-test: runs in a worker on demand and when the engine hash is new, stamped with the engine hash. Results from an engine hash with no passing self-test are "unverified engine".
13.15 Versioning: results store engine hash and schema version. Results from another engine hash are "foreign", viewable but not reused for gap comparison or certification evidence.
13.16 Environment: required Web Workers from Blob, IndexedDB, typed arrays, Blob download, TextDecoder, Intl. Optional File System Access, DecompressionStream. Missing required capability shows an unsupported-environment screen. Supported browsers are decided by Phase 0 results.

SECTION 14. VERIFICATION AND VALIDATION
======================================================================

14.1 Method
- Fail-first: every defect fix starts with a failing test, captured output and a control case that passes before and after. The legacy suite is append-only. No test framework is added.
- Traceability: each test names the requirement identifiers it covers. The build fails if a numbered requirement has no covering test or is not marked "not testable" with a reason.
- Oracle: a separately implemented reference (outside the app, another language, written blind from the specification, 20.2) generates expected values for the published test pack. The engine matches within pre-registered tolerances.
- Holdout pack: a sealed set of cases authored by the Oracle Engineer that developers never see, run only by the Gate (22.2). Failures on it block approval.
- Tolerances and test parameters are visible, fixed before running, seeded. Test vectors are data and never feed the engine.

14.2 Tests (T-numbers are referenced by the task cards in Section 23)
Flow and policy:
(1) Number Policy and external-reference scans pass.
(2) Every enumerated flow transition is tested, a later stage cannot open until its predecessor is Done, a cancelled run does not complete a stage, an infeasible result blocks the next stage.

SECTION 19. AGENT TEAM
======================================================================

19.1 Principles
- The Product Owner (PO) is a human, the WFM planner, and is the only authority on business rules, requirement changes, waivers and release.
- Every agent works from a card. An agent does only what its card allows and stops and escalates when a task needs something its card forbids.
- Authors never review their own work. Reviewers never fix what they review. Approvers never author.
- No agent's statement about a test result is evidence. Evidence is what the gate pipeline reproduces from a clean checkout.
- Disagreement between agents is resolved by the authority matrix (19.12), then by the PO.

19.2 Instance naming and context isolation
- Instances are named ROLE-letter (for example COR-A, COR-B, CH-M-A). Each instance has its own context. It sees only the context pack assembled by the Orchestrator for the task (27.1).
- The two-instance rule for Tier 1: the instance that writes the specification and fixtures plan and the instance that implements are different (for example COR-A specifies and COR-B implements).
- An instance that authored an artifact in a task cannot be a verifier, reviewer, challenger or approver of that task.
- Reviewers, challengers and auditors never receive the author's reasoning or self-assessment, only artifacts.

19.3 Standard agent prompt skeleton (every card is filled into these ten fields)
1 IDENTITY: role ID and name.
2 MISSION: one sentence.
3 AUTHORITY: what the agent may decide or change.
4 PROHIBITIONS: what the agent must never do (including the independence rules).
5 INPUTS ALLOWED / FORBIDDEN: the artifact classes it may and may not read.
6 OUTPUTS: the artifacts and messages it must produce (formats in Section 27).
7 PROCEDURE: the ordered steps for a task.
8 STOP CONDITIONS: when to stop and escalate (missing input, conflicting instruction, out-of-card request, ambiguity in a requirement).
9 QUALITY CHECK BEFORE SUBMITTING: a checklist the agent completes and attaches.
10 SKILLS: skill IDs from Section 26 the agent is qualified in.
General stop rule for every agent: if a requirement is ambiguous or two requirements conflict, do not guess. Raise a Finding of type SPEC-AMBIGUITY (27.3) to the BA and continue only on parts not affected.

19.4 Human authority
PO. Decides business rules, requirement changes, waivers (Medium and Low only, in writing), escalations, release and which models fill which roles. Attends slice demos with anonymized real data. Cannot waive a Critical or High defect.

19.9 Challenger team
Mission of the team: try to disprove each solution, specification and test suite before it is accepted. The team never approves anything. It produces counterexamples and attack logs.

19.9.1 Members
CHL Challenge Lead.
 Mission: coordinate coverage.
 Authority: assign challengers by attack taxonomy for each task, merge attack logs, decide when coverage is complete, report coverage to the Gate.
 Prohibited: approving, editing artifacts, suppressing a finding.
 Procedure: read the task card, select attack codes from the taxonomy (19.9.2), assign one challenger per domain code, require the blind round, run the merge round, verify every finding has a reproducible counterexample or is marked unverified, deliver the merged Attack Log.
 Skills: SK-ADV, SK-GOV, SK-PM.
CH-M Mathematical Challenger. Attacks mathematics: queueing and statistics assumptions, numerical stability, bias, multiple testing, optimization claims. Looks for counterexamples to every stated bound, monotonicity or minimality claim and proposes alternative algorithms. Attack codes: A01, A02, A07, A12. Skills: SK-QUE, SK-STAT, SK-NUM, SK-LP, SK-ADV.
CH-W WFM Operations Challenger. Attacks meaning and plausibility: KPI definitions, labels, bridge semantics, planner workflows. Builds realistic scenarios (24/7 operation, cross-midnight shifts, hour-reduction weeks, low-volume nights, queues with different SLAs) and checks the results make operational sense. Attack codes: A05, A08, A12. Skills: SK-WFM, SK-ADV, SK-QUE (working level).
CH-S Software and Security Challenger. Attacks state, concurrency, determinism, performance and security: illegal transition sequences, cancel and resume, worker invariance, memory limits, injection, hostile files. Attack codes: A04, A06, A10, A11. Skills: SK-SEC, SK-PERF, SK-WRK, SK-STATE, SK-ADV.
CH-D Data Challenger. Generates hostile and awkward inputs: encodings, delimiters, ambiguous dates, huge files, mapping traps, duplicates that collide after removal, negatives that create gaps. Attack codes: A01, A03. Skills: SK-CSV, SK-DATA, SK-ADV.
CH-R Roster and Constraint Challenger. Tries to find a roster cheaper than a claimed minimum on small cases by brute force, tries to get invalid rosters past the validator, and probes rule interactions and infeasibility. Attack codes: A01, A05. Skills: SK-OR, SK-LP, SK-WFM, SK-ADV.
CH-X Process and Gaming Red Team. Attacks the process: tries to pass the gate with weak tests, hunts ambiguity and hidden assumptions in specifications, checks that labels like "certified" are never misused, inspects diffs of protected paths, checks that other reviewers' sign-offs match the artifact hashes. Attack codes: A09, A12. Skills: SK-GOV, SK-ADV, SK-TEST, SK-REQ.

19.9.2 Attack taxonomy (referenced by task cards)
A01 Boundary and degenerate values (zero, one, maximum, empty, single interval).
A02 Numerical limits (overflow, cancellation, tolerances, special-function ranges).
A03 Adversarial input files (encodings, delimiters, malformed, huge, ambiguous).
A04 State-machine abuse and ordering (illegal sequences, stale results presented as current).
A05 Rule interaction and infeasibility (rules that combine in unexpected ways).
A06 Determinism and concurrency (worker count, cancel and resume, ordering).
A07 Statistical validity (bias, multiple testing, leakage, optional stopping).
A08 WFM counterexamples (implausible or misleading results, KPI meaning).
A09 Gaming and label integrity (weak tests, hard-coded values, misleading labels, tolerance changes).
A10 Performance extremes (largest horizon, memory growth, stalled solver).
A11 Security and injection (XSS, prototype pollution, hostile project files).
A12 Specification ambiguity and hidden assumptions.

19.9.3 Operating model
1 Pre-mortem (design stage): each assigned challenger independently, without seeing the others, writes the top failure modes for the specification and the tests that would catch them. CHL merges. The developer must add tests for accepted failure modes.
2 Test-adequacy challenge (before implementation): challengers review the committed failing tests for missing cases and weak assertions. TST adds mutants to confirm the tests fail on planted defects.
3 Attack (after implementation): each challenger runs attacks in its codes against the implementation and produces an Attack Log (27.4): the attack, input, expected, observed, result.
4 Regression attack: after fixes, the same attacks are rerun and new variants tried.
5 Release sweep (Phase 10): the whole team attacks the integrated product on the case pack and on new adversarial cases.
Rules:
- Blind round first, merge round second, so challengers do not anchor on each other.
- A finding is valid only with a reproducible counterexample (input file, command, expected versus actual) or a formal argument that an auditor verifies. Otherwise it is marked unverified and tracked but does not block.
- AUD and the owner triage each finding as valid, invalid or duplicate in the time box set by the PO. The false-positive rate of each challenger is tracked.
- "No finding" is accepted only with a complete Attack Log covering every assigned code. Challengers must try to break the work, not review its presentation.
- Challengers use varied strategies (different prompts, seeds, models where available) to reduce shared blind spots.
- A challenger who authored or implemented any part of a task is excluded from it.
Coverage rule: the task card lists attack codes. For Tier 1 every listed code must be covered by at least one challenger, CH-S and CH-X are always assigned, and the CHL certifies coverage. The minimum number of independent challengers for Tier 1 is a PO parameter (Appendix B).

19.10 Gate roles
GATE Release Gate. Part one is the gate pipeline, a deterministic script that checks the evidence list (22.1) from a clean checkout and returns PASS or REJECT with the unmet item. Part two is the Gate Agent for judgment items. Default is REJECT until each item has evidence. Cites the unmet item for every rejection. Cannot be overridden by any agent. Skills: SK-GOV, SK-REQ, SK-TEST.

19.11 Qualification
Before first use and at each phase boundary:
- Reviewing roles (WFM, AUD-C, AUD-P, every challenger, VAL, ORA, Gate Agent) pass a seeded-defect exercise: a prepared package with planted defects in their domain (math error, rule violation, silent transformation, hidden assumption, misleading label, determinism break). Detection and false-positive rates are recorded. A role under the PO's threshold may not approve or clear anything until fixed.
- Developer roles pass a reference exercise: implement a specified function set to oracle fixtures without protected-path edits.
- Orchestrator passes a scheduling exercise with planted dependency and independence traps.
- Each skill ID in Section 26 has its proof exercise, and a role holds a skill only after passing it.

19.12 Authority matrix (D design, I implement, V verify, R review, C challenge, A approve, X may not)
Requirement and acceptance criteria: BA D; WFM R; ARC R; CH-X C; PO A.
Requirement change: BA D; ARC, WFM R; CH-X, CH-W C; PO A.
ADR: ARC D; challenger team C; AUD-C and AUD-P R; ARC A after clearance.
WFM formula or KPI definition: WFM, DOC D; ORA V; CH-W and CH-M C; PO A on dispute.
Module specification (Tier 1): developer D; ARC, WFM (if WFM meaning) R; challenger team C; ARC A.
Oracle values, case pack: ORA D; WFM R; PO A; developers X.
Code: developer I; TST V; AUD R; challenger team C; GATE A.
Validator and KPI recalculation: VAL D, I; ORA V; AUD-C R; GATE A; roster developers X.
Test or tolerance change: TST proposes; BA, ARC, WFM approve; logged; developers X.
Defect severity: AUD proposes; WFM confirms WFM impact; PO final.
Waiver (Medium, Low): PO A. Critical, High: never.
Phase exit: ORC V; GATE A; PO A. Release: GATE A, PO A, SEC V.

======================================================================
SECTION 20. INDEPENDENCE AND ANTI-GAMING
======================================================================

20.1 Independence by construction: author, reviewer, challenger and approver are different instances with separate contexts; reviewers and challengers receive artifacts only; AUD and the challenger team run on a different model from the task's implementers where the PO has more than one available, otherwise with separate contexts, blind prompts and mandatory qualification (19.11); sign-offs are bound to artifact hashes and any change invalidates them.
20.2 Blind implementation: ORA builds the oracle from specifications in a different language without seeing the engine; VAL builds the validator and KPI recalculation from rule text without seeing the generator, LP or DES; developers never see the sealed holdout pack, oracle source or validator source. An engine-oracle disagreement is a finding decided by ARC and WFM, and the decision is recorded.
20.3 Anti-gaming controls: protected paths (oracle, case pack, holdout pack, tolerances file, gate scripts, CI configuration) are read-only for developers; no deletion or weakening of tests, no loosened tolerance, no hard-coded expected values, no special-casing of test inputs; the gate diffs tests and tolerances against the approved baseline; fresh-checkout execution is the only evidence; Tier 1 results run twice and hashes must match; mutation checks for Tier 1 modules must kill planted mutants above the PO-set rate; the holdout pack must pass unseen.
20.4 Data protection: agent workspaces use synthetic or anonymized data only. Real operational data stays with the PO and is used only in slice demos run by the PO.

======================================================================
SECTION 21. TASK GOVERNANCE
======================================================================

21.1 Fundamental rule
A task that depends on another cannot start until that task is Closed. Independent tasks run in parallel when ARC approves their interfaces. ORC limits work in progress.

21.2 Risk tiers and team composition (tier set by BA and ARC at intake)
Tier 1 Critical: mathematics, DES, solvers, certification, roster generation, validator, cleaning, flow and fingerprints, hashing, container, determinism, and anything changing a staffing number or label. Team: spec owner and implementer (two instances), TST, ORA (where oracle applies), ARC design review, WFM (if WFM meaning), AUD-C and AUD-P as applicable, challengers per the coverage rule with CHL, Gate.
Tier 2 Core: ingestion screens, persistence, workers, exports, reporting numbers, performance, build scripts. Team: one owner instance, TST, one auditor, challengers on a sample chosen by ORC, Gate.
Tier 3 Minor: layout, navigation, wording not changing meaning, documentation. Team: owner, peer reviewer from FE or DOC, automated gate checks, BA or WFM only if KPI wording is touched.
The tier cannot be lowered without ARC and BA agreement.

21.3 Task states and lifecycle
States: Draft, Specified, Designed, TestsWritten, Implemented, Verified, Reviewed, Gated, Closed, plus Rejected and Blocked.
Transitions (who triggers): Draft to Specified (spec owner, BA confirms criteria); Specified to Designed (ARC, plus challenger pre-mortem for Tier 1); Designed to TestsWritten (TST and ORA data, failing run captured); TestsWritten to Implemented (implementer, quality checklist attached); Implemented to Verified (TST, VAL or ORA comparisons, determinism double-run); Verified to Reviewed (AUD, challenger Attack Log, WFM or BA sign-off where required); Reviewed to Gated (pipeline and Gate Agent); Gated to Closed (ORC records evidence, regression tests added, metrics updated). A rejection returns the task to the state where the defect originated (Specified, TestsWritten or Implemented) and invalidates later sign-offs. Blocked is set by ORC with a reason when a dependency or decision is missing.

21.4 Task card template (every task in Section 23 uses these fields)
ID, title, tier, Refs (Part A requirement IDs), Needs (task IDs), Spec (specification owner), Impl (implementer), Verify, Review, Challenge (with attack codes), Sign-offs, Skills (IDs), Inputs, Outputs, Acceptance (named tests and criteria), Attack focus, Risks.
For Tier 2 and 3, Spec and Impl are the same instance.

21.5 Rejection, rework and escalation
A rejection records the finding, severity, requirement ID and cited criterion, and returns the task. Affected tests and sign-offs are re-run. After the PO-set number of rejections on one task, ARC performs a root-cause review (specification, design or assignment). After a further PO-set number, the PO decides to change design, reassign, change the requirement through change control, or stop.

21.6 Severity
Critical: wrong staffing result, mathematical error, invalid roster, data loss or silent data change, security breach, nondeterminism, misleading label. High: major feature failure, incorrect KPI, violated Hard rule, broken flow gate. Medium: limited functional defect, incomplete validation. Low: usability, documentation. Critical and High block approval and cannot be waived. Medium and Low only by the PO in writing.

SECTION 22. THE GATE
======================================================================

22.1 Required evidence (any missing item is a REJECT citing the item)
G1 Task card, specification and acceptance criteria with hashes, mapped to requirement IDs.
G2 Approved specification and design sign-offs for the tier, bound to hashes.
G3 Failing-test evidence from before implementation, and the same tests passing from a clean checkout run by the pipeline.
G4 Every acceptance criterion mapped to a named test, and the traceability check passing.
G5 Oracle comparison within pre-registered tolerances (where applicable) and the holdout pack passing.
G6 No unapproved change to tests, tolerances, oracle or gate scripts.
G7 Static scans passing (Number Policy, external references, forbidden APIs in the engine core, innerHTML on data, circular dependencies).
G8 Determinism double-run identical, worker-count invariance where applicable.
G9 Mutation check at or above the PO-set kill rate (Tier 1).
G10 Audit report with artifacts and commands listed, and a complete Attack Log with coverage certified by the CHL (Tier 1 and sampled Tier 2), with all findings resolved or dispositioned by severity.
G11 WFM sign-off where WFM meaning is touched, BA sign-off for acceptance criteria.
G12 No open Critical or High defect, PO waivers recorded for Medium and Low.
G13 Performance within the approved budget where one exists.
G14 Documentation and changelog updated, regression tests added.
G15 All sign-offs bound to the current artifact hashes.

22.2 Behavior
The pipeline checks G1 to G9 and G12 to G15 mechanically. The Gate Agent handles G10 and G11. Default is REJECT. It rejects only by citing the unmet item, must not invent criteria, and must approve when all items are met. Decisions, evidence lists and hashes are stored and visible to the PO. Rejection reasons are tallied to detect both rubber-stamping and obstruction.

SECTION 24. PROGRAM MANAGEMENT
======================================================================

24.1 Change control: every requirement change is a change request written by BA with an impact analysis (requirements, tasks, tests, phases), reviewed by ARC and WFM, challenged by CH-X and CH-W, and decided by the PO. Approved changes increment the PRD version, update traceability and mark affected tasks and sign-offs stale. No agent changes requirements, tolerances or protected files outside this process.
24.2 Traceability: every Part A numbered item maps to tasks and tests, and the build fails on an uncovered requirement.
24.3 Metrics (reviewed at each retrospective): reviewer and challenger detection rate on seeded defects; challenger false-positive rate; defect escape rate (found after the gate); rework cycles per task; gate rejection reasons including rejections without a valid citation; flaky tests; mutation kill rate; time in review; open Critical and High count; requirement coverage; attack-code coverage by Tier 1 task. A metric outside the PO's threshold triggers a recorded process change.
24.4 Release criteria: all phase exits met; all Section 14 tests, nine cases and the holdout pack passing; no open Critical or High defect; every waiver recorded by the PO; traceability complete; WFM, technical and business acceptance present; challenger release sweep complete with all findings dispositioned; offline delivery verified; final HTML hash recorded; the Gate approves and the PO signs.

SECTION 27. COMMUNICATION CONTRACTS
======================================================================

27.1 Task Packet (ORC to an agent): task card (21.4); the context pack (only the allowed artifact classes, with hashes); the role card and prompt skeleton (19.3); the acceptance criteria; deadlines and stop conditions.
27.2 Evidence Package (agent to ORC): task ID and role instance; artifact hashes read and produced; commands run with their outputs (never summaries); the quality checklist with each item marked; open questions; verdict if the role gives one.
27.3 Finding Report: finding ID; type (DEFECT, SPEC-AMBIGUITY, PROCESS, TEST-GAP); severity (21.6); requirement ID; evidence (reproducible input, command, expected, observed, or a formal argument); suggested test; status (open, valid, invalid, duplicate, fixed, verified).
27.4 Attack Log: task ID; challenger instance; for each attack: attack code, attack description, input, expected, observed, result (survived or broke); coverage statement listing assigned codes.
27.5 Sign-off Record: role instance; task; artifact hashes reviewed; verdict (APPROVE, REJECT, APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS where conditions are Low or Medium only); cited criteria; date. Invalid when any hash changes.
27.6 Escalation Message: task ID; reason (ambiguity, conflict, independence violation, repeated rejection, missing input); options considered; the decision needed and from whom.
27.7 Rules: messages are plain text in these formats; free-form claims without evidence are ignored; a message that omits required fields is returned unprocessed.
